Asia Markets Briefing — 12 Oct 2026

South Korea’s chip-led sell-off set the pace in Asia: the Korea Composite Stock Price Index (KOSPI) was down 3.02% at 6,426 by early afternoon, with foreign and institutional sales concentrated in Samsung Electronics and SK hynix. Investors were reassessing AI demand after questions about OpenAI’s reported revenue and Friday’s weakness in US memory shares; Samsung’s preliminary third-quarter operating profit of 107.4 trillion won also undershot the 112.73 trillion won estimate compiled by Visible Alpha, although it beat some other forecasts. Renewed attacks on Saudi Arabia added an energy and inflation risk to the technology rout: Brent stood at $103.33 a barrel, up 1.33%, around midday in Asia. LG Innotek tumbled 11.71% as brokers cut earnings estimates amid the stronger won and higher input costs. The won itself touched a two-year high amid corporate dollar sales.

Hong Kong’s early rebound narrowed sharply by midday, leaving the Hang Seng Index up 0.17% at 24,254; the CSI 300 Index was off 0.15% at 4,311. Xiaomi pulled back from Friday’s rally, while mainland banks rose against a weaker A-share market. Industrial and Commercial Bank of China, Bank of China and China Construction Bank reached intraday records as analysts pointed to new housing-credit support, cheaper central-bank refinancing and bigger interim dividends. A new nationwide mortgage programme offering subsidised first-home borrowing underpinned the banks’ rally. Friday’s talks in Beijing yielded a separate China–EU understanding to moderate hybrid and plug-in-hybrid exports: the EU trade commissioner said it could more than halve shipments over four years relative to unchanged-policy projections, but implementation still requires European approval.

Taiwan’s TAIEX was little changed, but electronics and shipping stocks moved decisively. Pegatron gained 9.92% after September revenue reached NT$214.75 billion, up 86.88% from a year earlier, as flagship-phone shipments and AI-server assembly bolstered the contractor’s outlook. Evergreen Marine Taiwan climbed 9.89% after management said the container-shipping peak season had extended beyond September and bookings ahead of November looked strong; September revenue was up 63.75% year on year. Elsewhere, the Jakarta Composite Index fell 1.76% to 5,987 by late morning. In India the rupee rallied after the central bank tightened access to speculative currency derivatives and opened a special dollar window to meet the daily needs of three state oil companies, a direct response to pressure on the currency even as local equities slipped modestly.
